**AI-Generated and Student Journalism: Multidimensional Register Analysis**

**Project overview**
This repository accompanies the study “AI-generated vs student journalism in higher education: A multidimensional register analysis for AI literacy and writing pedagogy.”
The study compares the register profiles of university-level student-authored news and feature articles with corresponding texts generated by ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot, and Google Gemini. Using Biber’s multidimensional framework, implemented through the Multidimensional Analysis Tagger (MAT), it examines dimension-level register differences and agreement between the closest text-type classifications assigned to matched student-authored and AI-generated articles.

**Scope of the public deposit**
The public deposit provides the tabulated results used to report corpus composition, register profiles, statistical comparisons, text-type classifications, and mimicry rates. It is a collection of analytical results rather than the complete underlying textual corpus.

**Interpretation of results**
Dimension-level comparisons and text-type mimicry describe different aspects of register approximation. Dimension scores indicate how texts are positioned along the register dimensions examined in the study. Text-type mimicry records whether a student-authored article and its AI-generated counterpart receive the same closest MAT text-type classification.
These measures should not be interpreted as direct assessments of factual accuracy, overall writing quality, or authorship certainty. The results should be read alongside the accompanying article, particularly its descriptions of corpus construction, prompting procedures, statistical analysis, and study limitations.Restricted textual materials and access requests

**Restricted textual materials and access requests**
The original student-authored articles and their AI-generated counterparts are not included in this public repository. These textual materials are withheld to address privacy concerns and because their public release has not been authorized by the editorial board of the student publication. Authorization to use the materials for the study does not constitute authorization for their public redistribution.
The textual materials may be made available upon reasonable request to the corresponding researcher. Requests should explain the intended use of the materials, the specific files required, and how privacy and confidentiality will be protected. Access is not automatic and remains subject to review and any necessary authorization from the student publication’s editorial board.
